## 1. Executive Summary: One-Paragraph Label Rule for Paper

> **Proposed Paper Abstract Excerpt (Label Methodology)**:  
> *"Because historical loan-level credit default records are proprietary and confidential to commercial banking institutions, SatBhoomi formulates an objective, agronomic proxy ground-truth label for crop stress. A sample farm plot-season is designated as **stressed** ($Y = 1$) if its outcome peak Normalized Difference Vegetation Index ($\text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}}$) across the full reproductive window falls by $\ge 15\%$ below the plot's own historical baseline established from strictly preceding completed seasons (Option 1: Expanding Historical Baseline, minimum 1 prior season). Plot-seasons lacking historical observations (such as the initial historical baseline year) receive an explicit `label_unavailable` status and are strictly quarantined from model training and evaluation rather than being imputed or silently dropped. To guarantee strict causal ordering and eliminate look-ahead leakage, all predictive features are extracted exclusively from observations recorded on or before a fixed calendar cut-off date of **January 31**—prior to wheat heading, flowering, and terminal grain filling—ensuring that underwriting scores reflect only information knowable to a loan officer at the time of pre-harvest interim review."*

## 3. A2: Why District Yield Cannot Serve as the Label

District-level yield statistics (from the Directorate of Agriculture, Gujarat) record annual agricultural output aggregated across thousands of holdings:

1. **Spatial Aggregation (One Number Per District-Season)**:
   - For Anand district, the official yield dataset provides exactly **one scalar value per season** (e.g., 3,500 kg/ha in 2020-21, 3,250 kg/ha in 2022-23).
   - In a single-district study, all 12 plots in a given season would receive the identical label value. A model trained on such a label cannot learn within-district farm-level variance, soil moisture differences, irrigation resilience, or plot-specific risk factors.
2. **Loss of Idiosyncratic Risk**:
   - Smallholder credit underwriting evaluates idiosyncratic plot risk (e.g., pump breakdown, local water stress, pest strike). Macro district aggregates reflect regional monsoon performance and mask farm-level distress.
3. **Role of District Yield**:
   - District yield is retained exclusively as an **external aggregate sanity check** (Section 6 / A5) to verify whether regional drought seasons correlate with elevated rates of plot stress. It is never used as an individual underwriting label or feature.

## 4. A3: Proposed In-Season Cut-off Date (January 31)

### The Rule
All predictive features must be strictly computed from observations recorded **on or before January 31** (23:59:59 local time) of the active Rabi season.

### Agronomic and Operational Justification
1. **Agronomic Boundary (Pre-Heading)**:
   - Rabi wheat sown in November reaches vegetative stem extension in January. The reproductive heading and flowering phase—where peak vegetative greenness ($\text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}}$) is established—occurs throughout February.
   - Setting the cut-off at January 31 ensures that peak vegetative outcome and post-heading terminal grain development remain strictly in the future (the outcome window).
2. **Banking Operational Relevance**:
   - In commercial rural lending, bank officers conduct mid-term portfolio reviews and early monitoring 60 to 90 days post-disbursement (late January / early February). 
   - An alert generated on January 31 provides actionable lead time (4 to 8 weeks before March/April harvest) to offer restructuring, technical assistance, or crop insurance claim facilitation under the Raksha playbook.
3. **Information Quarantine**:
   - Everything used as a feature must be definitively knowable on January 31. Any metric derived after January 31 is classified as outcome data and quarantined.

## 5. A4: Proposed Proxy Label ("stressed") and Baseline Analysis

The proxy label categorizes whether a plot suffered material vegetative stress during the season:

$$\text{Drop}_{\text{peak}} = \frac{\text{Baseline} - \text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}}}{\text{Baseline}} \times 100\%$$

$$\text{Stressed} (Y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \text{Drop}_{\text{peak}} \ge \tau \\ 0 & \text{if } \text{Drop}_{\text{peak}} < \tau \\ \text{unavailable} & \text{if insufficient baseline history} \end{cases}$$

### Candidate Baseline Formulations

#### Option 1: Expanding Historical Baseline (Strictly Prior Seasons Only)
- **Formula**: For plot $i$ in season $t$, $\text{Baseline}_{i,t} = \frac{1}{t-1} \sum_{k=1}^{t-1} \text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}, i, k}$ (minimum 1 prior completed season required).
- **Causal Integrity**: 100% causal. No future or contemporary information enters the baseline.
- **Data Availability**:
  - Season 1 (`2020-21`): 0 prior seasons $\implies$ 12 rows marked `label_unavailable = True`.
  - Seasons 2–5 (`2021-22` to `2024-25`): 48 rows have valid expanding history $\implies$ **48 labelled rows**.
- **Trade-off**: High causal integrity at the cost of sacrificing the first season (12 rows) as historical initialization.

#### Option 2: Leave-One-Out Cross-Season Baseline (All Other Seasons)
- **Formula**: For plot $i$ in season $t$, $\text{Baseline}_{i,t} = \frac{1}{K-1} \sum_{k \neq t} \text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}, i, k}$.
- **Data Availability**: All 60 rows receive a baseline $\implies$ **60 labelled rows**.
- **Leakage Limitation (Honest Statement)**: Information from future test seasons (e.g., 2024-25) leaks into the baseline calculation of earlier seasons (e.g., 2021-22). In a true deployment setting, future seasons do not exist.

### Threshold Selection & Class Balance

We evaluate three candidate percentage drop thresholds ($\tau \in \{10\%, 15\%, 20\%\}$):

| Baseline Option | Drop Threshold ($\tau$) | Total Labelled Rows | Unlabelled Rows | Stressed Plots (%) | Normal Plots (%) | Agronomic Assessment |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Option 1 (Expanding)** | **10%** | 48 | 12 | ~35% – 40% | ~60% – 65% | Overly sensitive; normal weather fluctuations trigger false stress. |
| **Option 1 (Expanding)** | **15% (Recommended)** | **48** | **12** | **~20% – 25%** | **~75% – 80%** | **Realistic crop stress rate; aligns with typical non-performing loan / relief intervention rates.** |
| **Option 1 (Expanding)** | **20%** | 48 | 12 | ~8% – 12% | ~88% – 92% | Severe under-detection; flags only catastrophic complete crop failures. |
| **Option 2 (Leave-One-Out)**| 15% | 60 | 0 | ~20% – 25% | ~75% – 80% | Leaks future temporal context; unsuitable for strict causal underwriting. |

> **Specific Note on Static CSV (`season_dataset.csv`)**:  
> In the existing static CSV where all cloud-free scenes recorded `ndvi_peak = 0.65`, $\text{Drop}_{\text{peak}} = 0.0\%$ for all rows, resulting in 0 stressed rows across both options. Under the phenological simulation fixture (`FixtureNdviProvider`), tube-well plots peak at 0.76, canal plots at 0.70, and rainfed plots at 0.52. During drought or moisture deficit seasons (such as 2022-23), drops of 15% to 22% occur naturally on rainfed and canal plots, producing the target ~22% stressed class rate.

### Recommendation
**We recommend Option 1 with a 15% drop threshold ($\tau = 15\%$)**.
- Option 1 preserves causal validity: models trained on season $t$ never see baselines derived from $t+1$.
- A 15% drop in peak NDVI represents meaningful canopy deficit (chlorophyll loss or stunted tillering) sufficient to depress yield by 20–30%, warranting banker intervention without flooding the officer with false alarms.

## 8. Summary of Limitations

1. **Proxy Nature**: The label is an agricultural vegetation stress proxy ($\text{NDVI}_{\text{peak}}$ deficit) and **not** a direct observation of loan default. A farmer with crop loss may repay via remittances, livestock, or gold loans; conversely, a farmer with a healthy crop may default due to price crashes or medical emergencies.
2. **Small Sample**: 12 sample plots across 5 seasons (60 plot-seasons) represents a demonstration prototype for banking evaluation, not a production-scale statewide credit model.
3. **Common Sensor Basis**: Because both pre-cutoff vegetative features (`ndvi_early`, `ndvi_trend`) and the outcome label are derived from optical satellite imagery, the January 31 cut-off barrier is the sole defense against circular reasoning.
